◆ _computeFacesUniqueIdsParallelV1()

void Arcane::mesh::FaceUniqueIdBuilder::_computeFacesUniqueIdsParallelV1 ( )
private

Calculates the unique numbers for each face in parallel.

In addition to numbering, it determines the owning sub-domain of each face, considering that a face belongs to the same sub-domain as the cell behind it (or to the current sub-domain for a boundary cell).

A face is numbered from its back cell: the cells, in increasing uniqueId, each own a contiguous block of face ids, one per face they are behind plus one per face of the domain boundary they hold, and a face takes its position in the block. This is a global computation: a face's uniqueId depends on the cells of every sub-domain with a smaller uniqueId.

By default every rank performs that computation whole: it gathers the sub-domain boundary faces and then the cells of the entire mesh, and numbers the mesh sequentially to keep its own share (_v1ByAllGather()). With ARCANE_DISTRIBUTED_FACE_NUMBERING=1 the computation itself is distributed over the ranks (_v1Distributed()), with the same uniqueIds.

◆ _v1Distributed()

void Arcane::mesh::FaceUniqueIdBuilder::_v1Distributed ( )
private

Distributed computation of the face numbering of version 1.

Each rank computes a share of the numbering instead of all of it, and holds O(N/P + B) data instead of O(N), for N cells, P ranks and B faces on the boundary of its sub-domain; the number of collectives does not depend on N.

Two facts come from the other sub-domains: for a face with one cell here, whether a cell is on the other side and on which rank (if not, the face is on the boundary of the domain); and where every cell's block starts, which is the sum of the block sizes of all the cells of smaller uniqueId, a prefix sum over the whole mesh. Only the rank in front of a shared face needs both: the rank behind it numbers it from its own cell's block.

  1. Matching the faces, by a rendezvous. Each face with one cell here is sent to a rank that every holder of the face computes from the face alone, a hash of its smallest node uniqueId. The two halves of a shared face meet there and each learns the rank of the other. A face that arrives alone is on the domain boundary and gets no answer, but when its cell is in front of it (it then counts in that cell's block) the cell is passed on to the rank that computes the start of that cell's block.
  2. The block starts, by a parallel prefix sum over the cells in uniqueId order. The uniqueId range is cut in one slice per rank, at bounds taken from a histogram so that the slices hold the same amount of work. The cells of a rank form runs of consecutive uniqueIds, inside which no cell of another rank can sit, so a run travels as one record (first, count, back faces) to the rank of its slice. The totals of the slices are summed over the ranks while step 1 completes, which gives every slice its offset; a slice orders its runs, adds the boundary faces of step 1 and answers each run with its start, from which the owner of the run deduces the start of each of its cells.
  3. The shared faces. The rank behind a shared face sends its uniqueId to the rank in front; both list the faces they share in node order, so no key travels with the uniqueIds.

Step 2 is sent while step 1 travels. A mesh the numbering cannot describe (a face held by more than two sub-domains or behind on both, a cell held by two ranks or by none) is a fatal error: the gathering version numbers it inconsistently without a word.

Step 1 is inspired by the rendezvous algorithm of Plimpton, Hendrickson and Stewart (J. Parallel Distrib. Comput., 2004).
