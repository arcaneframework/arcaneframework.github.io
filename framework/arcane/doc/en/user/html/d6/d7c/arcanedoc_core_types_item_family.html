The Item Family

The IItemFamily interface (defined in arcane/core/IItemFamily.h) is the interface of an entity family. A family manages all the entities of the same kind (Item::kind()) and is attached to a mesh (IMesh, see The Mesh).

For any mesh, there is exactly one family of nodes (Node), one of edges (Edge), one of faces (Face), and one of cells (Cell). These entities are called base mesh entities and the associated families are the base mesh families.

Depending on the implementation, there may also be families of particles (Particle), dual nodes, links, or degrees of freedom (DoF). Depending on the requested connectivity, a family may not have elements. For example, by default in 3D, edges are not created.

The entity kinds are defined by the eItemKind enumeration: IK_Node, IK_Edge, IK_Face, IK_Cell, IK_DoF, IK_Particle.

Getting a Family

The families of a mesh are retrieved from the IMesh interface:

  • IMesh::nodeFamily(), IMesh::edgeFamily(), IMesh::faceFamily(), IMesh::cellFamily(): the base families,
  • IMesh::itemFamily(): the family of the base kind kind,
  • IMesh::findItemFamily(): the family named name (throws an exception if it does not exist),
  • IMesh::createItemFamily(): creates a new family,
  • IMesh::itemFamilies(): the collection of all families of the mesh.

A family provides its name (IItemFamily::name(), and IItemFamily::fullName() which includes the mesh name), its kind (IItemFamily::itemKind()), and its associated objects: IItemFamily::mesh(), IItemFamily::parallelMng(), IItemFamily::traceMng(), IItemFamily::policyMng() (the behaviors/policies manager), and IItemFamily::properties().

Entity Identifiers

Each entity in a family has two identifiers:

  • the local identifier, Item::localId(): the position of the entity within the family of the local subdomain. This identifier may be modified when the family evolves (addition, removal, or compaction of entities), and the local identifiers are not necessarily contiguous. IItemFamily::nbItem() gives the number of entities and IItemFamily::maxLocalId() the maximum local identifier (which is the size required to dimension variables on the family).
  • the unique identifier, Item::uniqueId(): stable across the computation and unique across all subdomains. It is the identifier to use to refer to an entity from one subdomain to another.

By default, a family has a conversion table from Item::uniqueId() to Item::localId(). This table must exist to allow:

  • the guarantee that the unique number is unique within the subdomain and across all subdomains,
  • the conversion methods IItemFamily::itemsUniqueIdToLocalId(),
  • the presence of the family entities in multiple subdomains,
  • the synchronizations,
  • the partial variables on the family.

It is possible to enable or disable this conversion table with IItemFamily::setHasUniqueIdMap() (and query it with IItemFamily::hasUniqueIdMap()), but only if no entity has been created, and not on the node, edge, face, and cell families.

To convert unique numbers to local numbers:

IItemFamily* family = mesh->cellFamily();
Int32Array local_ids(nb_unique);
family->itemsUniqueIdToLocalId(local_ids, unique_ids, true);
// local_ids[i] is the local identifier of the entity with unique number
// unique_ids[i], or NULL_ITEM_ID if not found (and a fatal error if
// do_fatal is true).
Warning
When a family is modified by adding or removing entities, the variables and groups relying on this family are no longer usable until IItemFamily::endUpdate() is called, and the entity instances (Item) are invalidated. To retain a reference to an entity, you must either use a group (ItemGroup) or keep its unique number and use IItemFamily::itemsUniqueIdToLocalId().

Groups and Variables on the Family

A family manages the groups and the variables of its entities:

  • IItemFamily::allItems(): the group of all the entities of the family,
  • IItemFamily::groups(): the collection of groups in the family,
  • IItemFamily::findGroup(): searches for a group (by name, optionally creating it if it does not exist),
  • IItemFamily::createGroup(): creates a group,
  • IItemFamily::destroyGroups(): deletes all the groups of the family,
  • IItemFamily::findVariable(): searches for the variable named name associated with the family,
  • IItemFamily::usedVariables(): adds the list of variables used by the family to a collection.

Variables defined on the entities of the family (such as the AXL-declared variables of a module on the cell family) are resized automatically when the family is updated.

Modifying the Entity List

After modifying the entity list (adding or removing entities), IItemFamily::endUpdate() must be called. It updates the groups and resizes the variables on the family.

For optimization purposes, when performing several successive modifications, it is possible to use the partial update methods (reserved for experienced users):

  • IItemFamily::partialEndUpdate(): updates the internal structures without updating the groups or variables. Only the allItems() group is available.
  • IItemFamily::partialEndUpdateGroup(): updates a single group after the modification (removes the entities that were destroyed).
  • IItemFamily::partialEndUpdateVariable(): resizes a single variable after the modification.

Other modification-related methods:

  • IItemFamily::clearItems(): deletes all the entities of the family (be careful not to destroy entities used by another family; it is generally safer to use IMeshModifier::clearItems() to delete all the elements of the mesh),
  • IItemFamily::compactItems(): compresses the entities so that the local identifiers are renumbered from 0 to nbItem()-1,
  • IItemFamily::setItemSortFunction() / IItemFamily::itemSortFunction(): sets the entity sorting function (by default, entities are sorted by ascending unique number),
  • IItemFamily::notifyItemsOwnerChanged(): notifies that the entities owned by the subdomain have changed,
  • IItemFamily::notifyItemsUniqueIdChanged(): notifies that the unique identifiers of the entities have changed,
  • IItemFamily::addGhostItems(): allocates ghost entities (the entities present on another subdomain); IItemFamily::endUpdate() must be called after the allocations,
  • IItemFamily::copyItemsValues() / IItemFamily::copyItemsMeanValues(): copies (or averages) the values of the entities of a list into the entities of another list,
  • IItemFamily::experimentalChangeUniqueId(): changes the unique number of an entity (experimental: only for entities not yet connected to others).

Synchronization and Ghost Entities

In parallel, the entities of a family can be shared by several subdomains (ghost entities). The synchronization of the variables between the subdomains is managed by the family:

  • IItemFamily::computeSynchronizeInfos(): constructs the structures necessary for the synchronization. This collective operation must be performed every time the entities change ownership (for example, during a load balancing),
  • IItemFamily::synchronize(): synchronizes the given variables (which must come from this family and must not be partial) on all the entities of the family, or on a list of entities,
  • IItemFamily::allItemsSynchronizer(): the synchronizer on all the entities of the family,
  • IItemFamily::reduceFromGhostItems(): applies a reduction operation from the ghost items (the inverse of a synchronization),
  • IItemFamily::getCommunicatingSubDomains(): the list of subdomains communicating for the entities,
  • IItemFamily::itemsNewOwner(): the variable containing the number of the new owning subdomain of the entities (used for mesh partitioning).

See Handling parallelism in Arcane for a general presentation of the synchronization in Arcane.

Adjacencies and Connectivity

The family provides methods to compute the adjacency lists:

  • IItemFamily::findAdjacencyItems(): searches for the list of entities of a given sub-kind, linked by a given link kind, over a number of layers. If the list does not exist, it is created.
  • IItemFamily::localConnectivityInfos() / IItemFamily::globalConnectivityInfos(): the information on the connectivity, local to the subdomain or global across all subdomains.

The connectivity between the entities of the mesh is described by the connectivity mechanism presented in Entity Connectivity Management.

Sub-mesh Nesting

A sub-mesh (see Primary and Secondary Meshes) has families nested with respect to the families of its parent mesh:

  • IItemFamily::parentFamily(): the parent family (or nullptr),
  • IItemFamily::childFamilies(): the child families,
  • IItemFamily::parentFamilyDepth(): the nesting depth of the current family,
  • IItemFamily::setParentFamily() / IItemFamily::addChildFamily(): set up the nesting (to be used before IItemFamily::build() for dynamically constructed sub-meshes).

Other Features

  • IItemFamily::view(): a view on the entities of the family (valid only as long as the family does not evolve),
  • IItemFamily::toParticleFamily() / IItemFamily::toDoFFamily(): convert the family to the IParticleFamily or IDoFFamily interface when the kind matches,
  • IItemFamily::checkValid() / IItemFamily::checkValidConnectivity(): check the validity of the internal structures,
  • IItemFamily::checkUniqueIds(): collectively checks that the given unique identifiers are truly unique for all subdomains,
  • IItemFamily::itemListChangedEvent(): an event notified when entities are added or removed,
  • IItemFamily::prepareForDump() / IItemFamily::readFromDump(): prepare or read the family data for a dump.

Key Points

  • A family is the per-mesh container of all the entities of one kind: it owns their numbering, their groups, their variables, and their synchronization.
  • The Item::localId() of an entity can change when the family evolves; the Item::uniqueId() is stable. Use the unique number (and IItemFamily::itemsUniqueIdToLocalId()) to refer to an entity across modifications or across subdomains.
  • After any modification of the entity list, call IItemFamily::endUpdate() (or the partial update methods) before using the groups and variables again.
  • For base mesh families (node, edge, face, cell), the unique-to-local conversion table is mandatory; for other families it can be disabled before any entity is created.
